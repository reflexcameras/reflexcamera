CAMERA CAPABILITIES / REFLEX SYSTEM

Key system features.

Purpose-built for recording fast-moving wildlife in complete darkness, Reflex cameras combine ultra-sensitive imaging, advanced motion detection and simple field setup in a powerful camera-trapping system for wildlife enthusiasts, researchers and ecological consultants. Position a camera near a roost or feature, power it up, and it will automatically record all of the action night after night.

Designed to detect brief, rapid movements that conventional camera traps miss, Reflex cameras capture high-quality footage of animals moving through the scene. They can operate over extended deployments, providing reliable video data across weeks or entire seasons rather than relying on a small number of individual survey visits.

Built-in live streaming makes positioning and setup quick and precise. Motion-triggered recording captures or tags key footage without filling storage with hours of empty video, while summary images provide an easy way to identify the moments that matter. Original high-quality video remains available for closer examination.

This combination makes Reflex cameras highly versatile for bat and wildlife surveys. They can be used to establish bat activity across a range of environments, including trees, hibernation sites, roof voids, buildings and caves. The cameras also excel at monitoring disturbance and exclusion activities during mitigation work.

Shared software across the range means the setup remains familiar whether you choose close-range infrared, ultra-sensitive infrared or wide-angle thermal.

BUILDING SURVEYS / RAPID DEPLOYMENT

Wide angle video

Wide-angle thermal and ultra-sensitive infrared can cover whole building elevations and show where bats emerge and how they move.

The Reflex Lynx's large, ultra-sensitive infrared sensor produces bright images with less illumination, helping detect bats at distance with less image noise and fewer areas lost in dark shadow.

The Reflex Pit Viper provides wide-angle thermal imaging with a 80° horizontal field of view. The video is 60 Hz at 640 x 480 resolution, allowing bats to be detected at distance without infared illumination.

Both the Reflex Lynx and Reflex Pit Viper cameras use advanced motion detection to pick out tiny changes between pixels, highlighting brief movements that the human eye can miss when watching at full speed.

The cameras can capture activity around large structures

TREE ROOSTS / CLOSE VIEW

At the roost entrance

Close infrared imagery can provide incredible detail which can be used for species identification and observing behaviour. Fast frame rates at 120 FPS provide detailed flight movements on emergence and re-entry.

ABOUT REFLEX / BRISTOL, UK

ABOUT REFLEX CAMERAS.

Designed and built by Sam Davis.

Reflex Cameras is a small, independent business based in Bristol run by bat worker Sam Davis.

Sam has worked with bats for over 12 years. The idea for Reflex cameras grew from the challenge of trying to monitor woodland bats; fast-moving animals that switch roosts regularly and have a habit of making survey plans difficult.

That led to the development of the first Reflex camera: a camera that could stay in place, keep watch and capture amazing moments that are easily missed.

Sam has spent the past nine years developing Reflex cameras, designing the hardware and software from the ground up and field-testing the cameras in local woodlands and around roosts. Each camera is hand-assembled in Bristol.

From tree roosts to wider surveys.

The adaptable hardware and software were originally designed for monitoring trees, but the cameras have since been used for many different types of monitoring, from loft voids to active mines. The range has now expanded with new infrared and thermal models for surveying buildings and larger areas.
